The First-Timer's Trade Show Kit: Banners, Posters and Handouts That Fill a 10x10 Booth

Four pieces, one clear message, and a booth that works from the aisle.

November 24, 2026

You've paid for the booth space. You've booked the travel. Now you're staring at an empty 10x10 square wondering what actually goes in it.

It's easy to overthink, and it's easy to overbuy. The good news is that a first-time booth doesn't need a truckload of gear. A small, well-chosen set of trade show booth materials will do the job: a banner to catch attention, a backdrop or poster to tell your story, handouts for people who stop, and a takeaway that gets them to follow up.

This guide walks through all four, with sizes, quantities, and the order to set them up.

What a 10x10 booth looks like

A standard 10x10 trade show booth is a 10-foot-wide, 10-foot-deep space, usually in a row of identical booths. In most shows you'll find:

  • A back wall (often 8 feet tall) behind you
  • Side walls (often 3 feet tall) separating you from your neighbors
  • An aisle in front, where all your traffic comes from
  • A 6-foot table (often available to rent, or you can bring your own)

That means you have three places to put your message: the back wall, the permitted display areas, and the table. Everything in your kit has a job in one of those zones.

Rules vary by event, so check your exhibitor manual for height limits, flame-retardant requirements, and any restrictions on hanging items.

1. The banner stand: your first impression

What it is: A tall, lightweight stand with a printed graphic that goes up in a minute or two. It's the one piece that nearly every booth has, and for good reason.

Your two main options:

  • Retractable banner: The print rolls up into a base and pulls up into a pole. Common size is around 33" x 80". It offers a polished, portable display. Confirm the hardware and carrying case details when requesting a quote.
  • X-frame banner: A lighter, simpler stand with a frame shaped like an X that holds the print at the corners. Sizes often run around 24" x 63" or similar. It's a lightweight, straightforward display option.

Which should you pick?

  • First show and a lean setup: X-frame, if permitted by your event
  • You'll use it again and again, and you want a premium look: retractable

What to put on it:

  • Your logo, large and clear
  • One headline that says what you do or offer, in about 6 to 8 words
  • A website, QR code, or short call to action at an easy-to-read and scan height

Keep the most important content between eye level, roughly 4 and 6 feet off the floor. Keep QR codes and important details above any furniture or hardware that could obscure them.

2. The backdrop or poster: tell your story

Your banner stops people. Your backdrop or poster tells them why they should stay.

Option A: A backdrop (back wall graphic) A large printed graphic across the back of your booth, often 8 to 10 feet wide, subject to event rules. Backdrop projects can be discussed through a custom printing inquiry; confirm material, hardware and availability before planning around one. It frames your space, reinforces your brand, and can make a plain booth look intentional. It's the best choice if you want a full, polished look.

Option B: A poster (on an easel or wall) A large-format poster, for example 24" x 36", displayed on an easel or mounted to the wall. It's easy and flexible. Use it for a product photo, a key offer, or a simple three-point explainer.

What to put on it:

  • One strong image or visual, not a wall of text
  • Your headline and logo
  • Three short benefits, or a simple "how it works" in 3 steps

A good test: can someone get the main idea in three seconds from 10 feet away? If not, cut words.

3. Table handouts: for the people who stop

Not everyone who walks past will talk to you. Handouts are for the ones who do.

Think in three tiers:

  • Flyers for quick grabs. A single sheet with your offer, what you do, and how to reach you. Typically 8.5" x 11" or a smaller size like 5.5" x 8.5". Great for volume.
  • Brochures for interested visitors who want details. A trifold or half-fold gives you room for services, process, and more. See our guide to standard brochure sizes and folds for the right format.
  • Catalogs for buyers with a product line to browse. If you have a lot of items, a short saddle-stitched catalog is the right tool. Our guide to perfect binding vs saddle stitch can help you choose.

How many to bring? There's no perfect number, but a starting point for a one-to-two day show is below. These are suggested distribution quantities, not Quixprint order quantities or minimums; confirm available quantities when requesting a quote.

  • Flyers: 250 to 500
  • Brochures: 100 to 250
  • Catalogs: 25 to 50 (hand these to serious prospects only)

Bring more if the show is large or runs multiple days. You can always carry leftovers to your next event, but you can't print more on the show floor.

Make them work together. Use the same colors, fonts, and key message across your banner, backdrop, and handouts so everything feels like one brand. And put a clear next step on every piece: a website, a QR code, or a short offer.

4. The takeaway: the thing they keep

Handouts get read. A takeaway gets kept.

A takeaway is a small, useful, or appealing item that gives someone a reason to remember you and a way to reach you. Good options for a first-timer:

  • A postcard or small card with a show-only offer and a QR code
  • A sticker with your logo, if your audience will actually use it
  • A business card with a memorable offer or follow-up link

Whatever you choose, make sure it has:

  1. Your name and logo
  2. A reason to act, such as a discount, free consultation, or giveaway entry
  3. A simple way to follow up, like a QR code, short URL, or phone number

A good takeaway also helps you track results. Use a unique link or code so you can see how many people came from the show.

How to decide what to bring

Not every show calls for every piece. Use this as a quick guide.

  • Smaller local show or first-ever booth: One X-frame banner, a poster, flyers, and a simple takeaway.
  • Larger industry show: Add a retractable banner or backdrop for a more polished look, plus brochures for serious prospects.
  • Product-heavy business: Add a short catalog for buyers who want to browse your range.
  • Service business: Focus on a strong banner and brochure that explain your process and results.

Whatever your mix, keep one rule in mind: every piece should support the same single message. It's better to have four consistent pieces than ten that don't match.

Setup order: how to build your booth

Put things up in this order, and the booth practically assembles itself.

  1. Check the space. Confirm your booth number, the back wall height, and where power and the table are.
  2. Install the approved backdrop (or set the poster). Start at the back, since it frames everything else.
  3. Place the table. Position it to one side or at an angle, leaving room for people to step in without feeling blocked.
  4. Set up the banner stand. Place it beside the table or in another permitted display area, inside your booth footprint and within event sightline rules.
  5. Arrange handouts and the takeaway. Flyers at the front edge, brochures a little behind, catalogs out of sight until needed. Put the takeaway where people can grab it easily.
  6. Step into the aisle and look back. Walk about 15 to 20 feet away and check what the booth says at a glance. Fix whatever's unclear.

Trade show prep timeline

  • 4 to 6 weeks before: Finalize your message and design. Order your banner, backdrop, and any large-format pieces.
  • 3 to 4 weeks before: Order flyers, brochures, catalogs, and the takeaway. Review proofs carefully.
  • 1 to 2 weeks before: Receive and check everything. Test the banner stand and pack a small repair kit with tape, scissors, and zip ties.
  • The day before: Pack your kit, handouts, and a few extras. Confirm your shipping or load-in details.

These are suggested planning windows, not guaranteed production or delivery times. Confirm your deadline, proof approval, shipping and production schedule when requesting a quote. Leave some buffer. If something arrives with an error, you'll want time to fix it.

File prep tips for trade show materials

  • Large-format files need less resolution. Banners and backdrops are viewed from a distance, so images can be lower resolution at full size than a flyer would need. Check your product's requirements.
  • Keep text away from the edges and bottom. Banner bottoms disappear into the base, and edges can be trimmed or tucked into hems.
  • Use CMYK and follow the product-specific bleed and safe-area requirements. Our guides on how to prepare files for printing and RGB vs CMYK cover the full checklist.
  • Test your QR codes. Scan every one on a phone before sending the file, and again on the finished print.

What this means for your business

A trade show isn't won by having the most stuff. It's won by being clear, consistent, and easy to talk to.

  • Start small and build. Four good pieces beat ten forgettable ones.
  • Say one thing well. A single message repeated across every piece is more memorable than a lot of different ones.
  • Make the next step obvious. Every banner, handout, and takeaway should point to the same action.
  • Plan for the follow-up. The show is only the beginning. The takeaway and your leads are what turn the weekend into sales.

Get your booth ready

Your first trade show doesn't need to be complicated. Choose a banner, a backdrop or poster, handouts, and a takeaway, then put them up in the right order.
